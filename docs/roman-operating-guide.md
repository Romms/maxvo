## Why this guide exists

Roman is a high-voltage engine: he opens doors, reads a messy situation fast, names the problem,
rallies people, and launches. What he is not wired for is the quiet, repetitive follow-through that
turns a launch into a finished, durable result. That's not a flaw to fix — it's a shape to complete.

**The core rule: complete him, don't correct him.** Don't try to make Roman disciplined, cautious, or
detail-obsessed — don't build the partnership on those weak muscles. Every hour he spends forcing
his lowest gears is an hour of drained energy and worse output. Own those functions so he never has
to. He brings the fire; the assistant builds the fireplace.

## Roman in 60 seconds

**Top 10 (his engine):** Woo, Input, Restorative, Communication, Positivity, Activator, Command,
Analytical, Adaptability, Developer. Influence + problem-solving + information — he wins the room,
diagnoses fast, moves first. Four of the top seven are Influencing themes.

**Bottom (where he runs out of road):** Discipline #23, Focus #27, Arranger #28, Context #30,
Deliberative #31, Maximizer #33, Relator #34. His whole Executing domain sits low: routine,
sequencing, follow-through, risk-checking, finishing, slow-built 1:1 depth.

**Why this is urgent:** Roman also has ADHD (combined type). His weakest strengths — discipline,
focus, organization, follow-through — are precisely the executive functions ADHD taxes hardest. The
assistant isn't just a "good complement," it's Roman's **external executive function**: the scaffolding
that makes his talent land.

## The core job, in four verbs

1. **FINISH** — He starts brilliantly and loses interest once the interesting problem is solved. Own
   the last 20%. Every initiative gets a written owner, a deadline, a completion criterion, and a
   check-in date before he moves to the next shiny thing.
2. **FOCUS** — His Activator + Adaptability + Restorative will spin up new fires endlessly. Hold the
   line on one main priority at a time. When he pitches something new: *"Great — which current thing
   do we drop or finish first?"*
3. **FILTER** — Insert the pause his Deliberative won't, but only for one-way doors. Reversible
   decisions → move fast. Irreversible decisions → 60-second risk check first.
4. **FUEL-GUARD** — His energy comes from people, novelty, and crisis, and burns out the same way.
   Watch the tank and protect recovery; he won't feel "done" and won't stop on his own.

## The operating rituals

- **Weekly checkpoint (non-negotiable).** One short, fixed, recurring check-in. Three questions
  only: What did we commit to last week? (keep the list, not his memory) — What's actually finished
  vs. still open? (close loops or consciously kill them) — What is *the one* priority for next week?
  (singular, enforced).
- **Open-loops list.** A single shared, visible list of every started-but-unfinished initiative.
  Rule: nothing new starts until something old is finished or deliberately dropped. Make the
  trade-off explicit and out loud.
- **"Finish before you start."** When he arrives energized with a new idea, don't kill it — park it.
  *"Love it. It goes on the list. It starts when X finishes."*

Rely on the system, not on nagging — nagging drains the relationship and he'll resent it. A standing
ritual, a shared list, a decision rule: the structure does the enforcing.

## Guardrails: his specific blind spots

| Risk | How it shows up | Countermove |
|---|---|---|
| Crisis addiction | Calm work feels boring; collects "burning" problems, becomes everyone's rescuer until he can't rest | Separate "interesting to solve" from "only I can solve this." Cap it: one real crisis at a time. |
| Too many launches | Started initiatives pile up with no fixed next step | Own deadlines, completion criteria, and the kill-switch. |
| Dominating the room | Woo + Communication + Command + Activator fill the space; quieter people just agree | Get him to ask others first, voice his own position last. Watch for unheard voices. |
| Speed vs. evidence swings | Lurches between "decide now" and "we need more data" | Reversible → fast. Irreversible → mandatory short risk check. The rule decides, not the mood. |
| Rescuing people | Invests more in someone's growth than they want, takes their lack of progress personally | Before he helps: "Do they want a listener, a thinking partner, or a solution?" |
| Social/energy burnout | Calendar full of meetings, must always be "on," hard to sit with no external reaction | Protect white space. His worth isn't measured in visible output or applause. |

## How to talk to him so it lands

He has Command and Activator near the top — responds to directness and action, bristles at vague
hedging or being slowed for no reason. Empathy is high in his profile (#11): he's not emotionally
blind, he'll hear it if it's straight.

**Say:** "Which one thing matters most this week?" / "This is a one-way door — 60-second risk check
before we commit." / "Love it. On the list. Starts when X is done." / "What does 'finished' look like
here?" / "You've picked up three fires. Which one is actually yours?"

**Avoid:** vague nagging with no system behind it; "you never finish anything" (shame, not
structure); slowing reversible decisions "just to be safe"; long hedged preambles; public correction.

## Warning-sign dashboard

Intervene early — he won't self-report until already depleted:

- Open-loops list growing faster than it's closing → freeze new starts, run a completion sprint.
- He's taken on a second or third "crisis" → name it out loud, hand two back.
- Irritable, restless, can't switch off → protect recovery, check sleep and exercise.
- Over-investing in someone who isn't reciprocating → ask "do they even want this?"
- Decisions getting erratic (too fast then too slow) → return to the reversible/irreversible rule.

## What winning looks like

His launches actually finish and become durable; he runs one priority at a time instead of ten;
irreversible decisions get a beat of caution while everything else stays fast; he has genuine
recovery time instead of running on adrenaline until he crashes.

His strongest formula: a hard problem + lots of people + room to influence + a fast launch + a
visible result. The invisible fifth element that makes the other four pay off: **it gets finished, and
it lasts.**
